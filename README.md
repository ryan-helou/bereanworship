Songs teach. A church that sings the same line every Sunday for a year has taught its people that line more thoroughly than most sermons will. I built BereanWorship so a worship leader can check a song before it goes on the set list, and see exactly why it earned the score it did. The name comes from the Bereans in Acts 17:11, who listened eagerly to what they were taught and still checked it against the Scriptures.

## What you can do with it

- Browse every reviewed song with its overall score out of 100 and its three parts: biblical soundness, how well a congregation can sing it, and how clear it is.
- Open a song to read the review: what it claims, the Scripture behind each claim, any concern found, and the sources used.
- Filter and sort by score, theme, genre or how easy it is to sing, and search by song, artist or album.
- See what a song gives a congregation through seven plain questions, such as whether it names God, states something God has done, and teaches doctrine and not only feeling.
- Keep private notes on each song and sort songs into Approved, Maybe and Rejected, so a team can make its own decisions beside the scores.
- Request a song that has not been reviewed yet.

## How a score is made

Every review is based on the actual words of the song, read in full. Each concern is written down with the claim it comes from, how serious it is and how central the doctrine is. A fixed formula then turns those findings into the score, so the same findings always give the same number, and any number on the site can be traced back to the finding behind it. Biblical soundness counts for 60 percent, congregational suitability for 20 percent and clarity for 20 percent.

A song is judged on its own words. Who wrote it never raises or lowers its score. A troubled ministry can write a sound song, and a sound one can write a careless song, so the two questions stay apart.

The site's methodology page lays out the full method, and the notes behind it are in the docs folder.

The live site sits behind a password.
